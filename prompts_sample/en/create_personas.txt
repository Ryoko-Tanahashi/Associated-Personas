Based on the provided data (Island Data), create **"one-to-one pairs of an agency representative and a system administrator in a specific trouble scenario."**

# Creation Process and Rules

## STEP 1: Extract Trouble Scenarios (Graph Analysis)
Analyze the input Issues and Relations, and split the data into **"independent trouble scenarios."**

* **Chain Merging (`isGeneratedBy`)**:
    * If Issue A is connected from Issue B by `isGeneratedBy`, treat this as a sequence of events happening to one agency representative. Group them into a single scenario.
    * Example: "Account locked" → "Unlock email does not arrive" = **one scenario**
* **Separate Unrelated Cases**:
    * Issues without a direct causal relationship (e.g., "forgot password" and "certificate error") must be treated as **different scenarios**.

## STEP 2: Create Paired Personas
For each extracted scenario, create the following **1:1 pair**.
Also collect the data basis `source_call_ids` used to build each persona.

### [User Side: Insurance Agency Representative]
* **Role**: The person directly facing that specific trouble scenario, whose work is blocked and who is in difficulty.
* **Knowledge Constraint**: They only know `abstract_issues` (problems) and `grouped_people` (emotions/background). **They do not know the solutions (`abstract_solutions`).**
* **Writing Policy (Narrative Style)**:
    * Do not list issues as simple bullet points. Instead, describe them as a coherent story (situation narrative): **"during what work, what happened, and what they tried next before getting stuck."**
    * If multiple Issues are included, express how they are intertwined over time or in combination to trouble this person.
* **Data Basis**: List `source_call_ids` linked to `abstract_issues` and corresponding `grouped_people` included in this scenario.

### [Support Side: Insurance Company System Administrator]
* **Role**: The person (operator/help desk) who receives that specific inquiry and leads it to resolution.
* **Granularity**: Do not depict a "super administrator who knows everything." Describe them as **"the specific person currently taking this call and handling the relevant Issue."**
* **Knowledge**: They know the concrete `abstract_solutions` (resolution procedures) for the Issues in that scenario.
* **Writing Policy (Manual Style)**:
    * Do not just enumerate solutions. Structure it as a **concrete response manual (or script) from receiving the inquiry to resolving it.**
    * If there are multiple Solutions, organize them into a **logical flow**, such as "first check A, if that fails guide B," or "let them choose C depending on the situation."
    * **Prohibited**: Do not include source IDs (e.g., AS1, AS19) inside the manual body. Also do not add solutions based on assumptions or general knowledge.
* **Data Basis**: List `source_call_ids` linked to `abstract_solutions` and corresponding `grouped_people` included in this scenario.

# Notes
- No solution leakage: Do not include resolution procedures or causes (Solution content) in `agent_persona`. They are in a state where they "do not know why it is not working." Also do not include descriptions that imply solutions. They are always acting based on what they believe is correct.
- Manual specificity: `admin_persona.response_manual` must be readable as an actual operator procedure, not just a feature list. However, do not add steps by assumption; construct it only from the provided `abstract_solutions`. Also, do not write a verbatim dialogue script—describe procedures only.
- Number of scenarios: Split into an appropriate number based on the input graph structure. Do not force everything into one pair.
